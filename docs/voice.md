# Voice

Optional voice uses `GEMINI_API_KEY` and `GEMINI_VOICE_ID` (and `GEMINI_TTS_MODEL` to choose another model), read on each call from the environment or the nearest `.env`. Voice is off until the speaker button in the message box is pressed; without the key or the voice, the button says what is missing. When Google refuses a voice call (a used-up quota, a refused key, a failed request), a warning notice in the conversation says so, and the mic or the speaker shows a dot with the reason until Google answers again. The server remembers Google's last refusal on each route for ten minutes, and `GET /api/voice` reports it as `warning` (on `hear` for hearing, at the top level for speech), so switching the mic or the speaker on says so at once. Start the host with `--disable-voice` to turn it off for good; every test launch does.

The server can also hear: `POST /api/voice/hear` turns a clip of speech (at most 20 seconds) into words with Google's `gemini-3.5-transcribe`, which needs only `GEMINI_API_KEY`. `GEMINI_HEAR_MODEL` chooses another model. Hearing costs about half a cent for each minute of speech, and the server caps what it will listen to: four clips at once, 90 seconds of audio in any minute, and `GEMINI_HEAR_DAILY_MINUTES` minutes a day (60 if unset, counted from midnight UTC). `REX_PROD_API=true cargo test -p agent --test hear_live -- --ignored --nocapture` speaks a sentence, a cough and some noise through Google and hears them back; it costs a few cents at most.

Talking to Jet: the microphone button sits beside the speaker in one pill at the right end of the message box. Press it and the browser asks for the microphone; the choice is kept per device in the browser (`jet.hear`), and on a later load Jet listens again by itself only if the browser has already allowed the microphone (otherwise one click on the mic starts it). While it listens, the page cuts what you say into utterances at natural pauses and sends each one as a clip of 16 kHz audio to `/api/voice/hear`, which asks Google's `gemini-3.5-transcribe` for its words. Nothing is sent while you are silent, and coughs, keyboard clatter and Jet's own voice are not sent as messages. The words land one at a time, with a capital at the start, in a faded bubble at the end of the conversation that appears with your first words, not in the message field; when you have finished a thought (a pause of 0.7 to 1.5 seconds, longer after words that sound unfinished) the bubble is sent in place as Enter would send it, so it turns solid with its time, and commands, attached files and the active app behave as they do for typing. A message spoken while Jet is still replying is held until the reply ends: the bubble leaves the conversation and the held chip shows it. In browsers with their own speech recognition, such as Chrome, your words also show in the bubble while you are still saying them, and Gemini's words take their place as they arrive; a message that sends itself holds only Gemini's words, except when Google could not hear you: the live words are then sent instead, and a warning notice says Jet is using the browser's own hearing until Google is back. In a browser with no speech recognition of its own, a clip that Google refuses twice stops the mic, with a dot, the reason in its tooltip and a warning notice that Jet can't hear right now. Send or Enter while the message field is empty sends the bubble at once as it stands, and the rest of that spoken turn is dropped, so nothing is sent twice. While the mic listens, a trail of bars runs across the empty message field, behind it, from the + button to the mic, and shows how loud the sound it hears is; the bars take the place of the "Listening…" text and stand tall only while you speak, so keys, breath and the room show as a low line. Typing in the field while you speak, or pressing Esc while the Send button glows, moves the bubble's words into the field before what you typed and stops that message from sending itself until the field is empty; heard words never land in the field unless you take over like this. With the speaker on as well the two make a conversation: speak over Jet and it goes quiet at once and your words become the next message; if the speakers make Jet hear itself twice, the microphone waits until Jet has finished speaking. After ten quiet minutes the microphone is let go and the mic shows it is paused; a click, typing or sending wakes it. The microphone is never opened without a click, or a remembered choice and a permission the browser already gave. Reduced motion hides the trail (the field says "Listening…" again) and makes words land whole. Only audio clips of speech go to Google through Jet, at the cost above, but while the mic listens the browser's own speech recognition also sends the microphone's sound to the browser maker's speech service (Google's in Chrome); with hearing off in the browser nothing is recorded or sent.
